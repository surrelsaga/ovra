64. **Playlist variety: an educated, research-backed rule instead of an impossible ideal (2026-11-16, built in Job 21; demo switches after 3 songs; a song counts as played when it ends, inferred within one 2 s poll):** what we wanted (variety across playlists *and* no Spotify pop-up) isn't possible with the Spotify desktop app on Free: picking a specific song always brings Spotify forward (decision 61), and Daniel rejected both frequent flashes and asking users to minimize Spotify or grant Accessibility. Silent skipping alone keeps the user in one playlist for too long. So we chose a deliberate, hardcoded rule, an educated guess grounded in what people report: during focused work many people keep one familiar playlist (familiar music is less distracting; people pick relaxing, familiar music for hard mental work; some dislike switching playlists every hour), but familiarity wears off and some want variety, and skipping itself can break flow. Rule: steer silently inside a playlist, then switch to another of the user's playlists (with a song that fits the mood) after a number of songs that depends on the activity: Focus and Reading ~12 songs (~40 min), Creative, Everyday tasks and Unwind ~6–8 songs (~25 min). Switch early when the mood needs songs the current playlist doesn't have; if none of the user's playlists fit, use Spotify's matching mood playlist (decision 31's fallback, now only at a switch). Each switch is one brief pop-up with a fast refocus, so ~1.5–2.5 per hour instead of every 3–4 min. Counts live in config so real use can tune them. Sources noted in the session (Chris Bailey on familiar music, a background-music study of 434 adults, Spotify Community and DEV Community threads, an older Spotify skip analysis of 14.65 skips/hour).
